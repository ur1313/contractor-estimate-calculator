How this is calculated

Billable hours = weeks × hours/week × billable %. Labor cost/hr = pay × (1 + burden) ÷ billable hours. Break-even = (labor cost + overhead) ÷ billable hours. Recommended rate = break-even ÷ (1 − profit margin). Burden % is a rough planning number. Self-employment tax alone is about 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings (IRS Topic 751), and insurance costs vary a lot by state and trade.

FAQ

How do I calculate my hourly rate as a contractor or handyman?

Add the pay you want, your labor burden and your yearly overhead, then divide by the hours you can actually bill (not every hour you work). Divide that break-even rate by (1 − your target profit margin) to get a rate that also leaves profit.

What is a billable hour?

An hour you can actually charge a client for. Driving between jobs, quoting, picking up supplies, bookkeeping and callbacks are real work but usually aren't billable, so most trades bill well under 100% of the hours they work.

What overhead should I include?

Everything you pay to stay in business that isn't tied to one job: vehicle, fuel, insurance, licenses, tools, phone, software, marketing, accounting and office costs. Divide your annual total by your billable hours.
