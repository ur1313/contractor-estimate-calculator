Formulas

Markup = (price − cost) ÷ cost. Margin = (price − cost) ÷ price. Price from markup = cost × (1 + markup). Price from margin = cost ÷ (1 − margin). Markup needed for a margin = margin ÷ (1 − margin).

FAQ

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is profit as a percentage of your cost. Margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price. A job that costs $1,000 and sells for $1,250 has a 25% markup but only a 20% margin.

What markup should a contractor use?

There is no single right number. It depends on your overhead, trade, market and job size. Work backward: figure out the gross margin your overhead and profit goal need, then convert it to a markup. For example, a 30% gross margin needs about a 42.9% markup on cost.

Why does a 50% markup only give a 33% margin?

Because margin is measured against the higher selling price. $100 cost × 1.5 = $150 price; the $50 profit is 50% of cost but only 33.3% of the $150 price.

How do I convert margin to markup?

Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin). A 25% margin needs a 33.3% markup; a 40% margin needs a 66.7% markup.
