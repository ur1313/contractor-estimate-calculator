How this is calculated

Billable hours = weeks × hours/week × billable %. Labor cost/hr = pay × (1 + burden) ÷ billable hours. Break-even = (labor cost + overhead) ÷ billable hours. Recommended rate = break-even ÷ (1 − profit margin). Burden % is a rough planning number. Self-employment tax alone is about 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings (IRS Topic 751), and insurance costs vary a lot by state and trade.

Formulas

Markup = (price − cost) ÷ cost. Margin = (price − cost) ÷ price. Price from markup = cost × (1 + markup). Price from margin = cost ÷ (1 − margin). Markup needed for a margin = margin ÷ (1 − margin).

FAQ

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is profit as a percentage of your cost. Margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price. A job that costs $1,000 and sells for $1,250 has a 25% markup but only a 20% margin.

What markup should a contractor use?

There is no single right number. It depends on your overhead, trade, market and job size. Work backward: figure out the gross margin your overhead and profit goal need, then convert it to a markup. For example, a 30% gross margin needs about a 42.9% markup on cost.

How do I calculate my hourly rate as a contractor or handyman?

Add the pay you want, your labor burden and your yearly overhead, then divide by the hours you can actually bill (not every hour you work). Divide that break-even rate by (1 − your target profit margin) to get a rate that also leaves profit.

Is this calculator free? Do I need an account?

Yes, it's free and there's no account. Everything runs in your browser and works offline after the page loads. Your inputs are saved only in your own browser.

Can I send the estimate to a client?

Yes. Use “Print / save client estimate” and pick Save as PDF. The client version shows selling prices only, never your costs or markup.
